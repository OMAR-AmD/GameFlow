Problem Domain & StatementDomain: Recommendation SystemProblem StatementWe are building a personalized video game recommendation engine for Steam users. Unlike conventional systems that recommend by genre tags, GameFlow recommends by behavioral profile — how a player actually plays (session intensity, completion ratio, competitive tendency, narrative affinity) rather than what genres they claim to prefer. The system targets gamers who struggle to discover relevant titles in a catalogue of 50,000+ games. It matters because genre-based filtering fails to distinguish two players who both own an RPG but play it in fundamentally different ways — one finishes every quest, the other explores freely and moves on. GameFlow captures that distinction and produces meaningfully differentiated recommendations for each player.2. DatasetDatasetSource URLSizeRoleSteam-200kkaggle.com/datasets/tamber/

18 columnsEnrichment — tags, genres, descriptions for content-based layerThe primary dataset provides three columns per interaction: user_id, game_title, and hours_played. Hours played is an implicit, honest behavioral signal far stronger than explicit ratings, which are sparse and biased. The enrichment dataset adds textual tags, genres, and descriptions, enabling the content-based filtering layer of the hybrid model. Note on data availability: Steam’s public API was restricted in 2019 — no public dataset contains granular per-session data. Session-level features will be constructed as justified proxies from playtime, a deliberate and documented feature engineering decision evaluated in Phase 3. The nikdavis metadata dataset supplies the median playtime per game required for completion_ratio, and the community tags (Multiplayer, Open World, Story Rich) needed for competitive_index, exploration_score, and narrative_affinity.3. SVD handles collaborative patterns in the sparse playtime matrix; TF-IDF CBF ensures recommendations for users with no history (cold start problem). PCA is applied prior to SVD training to reduce the dimensionality of the user–item matrix and extract dominant play-style patterns, mitigating the extreme sparsity inherent to Steam interaction data. The combination yields the best of both approaches.5.
